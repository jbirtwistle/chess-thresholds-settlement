The model, in one minute

In the last round each player either settles, steering toward a draw, or fights for a win. What a draw is worth compared with a win depends on prizes, title norms or qualification places, which are steps in the final score.

The model reduces each player's situation to one number, ρ: the share of the payoff step that lies below the draw. When ρ is close to 1 a draw secures almost everything and a win adds little. When ρ is close to 0 only a win matters. Without any cost of fighting, both players settle only if ρ₁ + ρ₂ > 1.

What the analysis would see if the model were true

These results come from 2,000 simulated Swiss tournaments of 60 players and 9 rounds. In the last round every player gets a personal target score, and the pair plays the equilibrium of the model. Nothing here is evidence about real players: the simulation reproduces the model's predictions by construction. It shows the pattern a real test should look for.

Share of simulated games drawn, by what each player needs

Needs: i only a draw, ii a win, iii nothing (secured or out of reach). Settings: prize 1, value of an extra point 0.05, cost of fighting 0.01; probability of a decisive game 0.25 if both settle, 0.45 if one fights, 0.75 if both fight.

Method and limits

  • Grand Swiss. Built from the PGN files of all 11 rounds: 638 games, 116 players, each playing once per round, and the points add up to 638.
  • Menorca. The round-9 table was checked against the final standings: 394 player results, no mismatches. That check also confirms round 9 was the last round.
  • Aeroflot. Round 9 was the final round. Names and ratings match the start list, apart from one footnote marker.
  • Stakes are proxied. The real thresholds in these events depend on final rank and tie-breaks, for example qualification places. This page does not code them. Distance to the leader stands in for them, which is crude.
  • A draw is not a settlement. The model is about choices, the data show results. A drawn game can come from a player who fought and could not win.
  • Strength matters. Stronger players draw more. Adjusting for average rating shrinks the estimated effect of being a top pairing (see the note under the chart), and the interval still includes no effect.
  • Small samples. The estimates use a stratified odds ratio (Mantel–Haenszel) so tournaments with different draw rates are not mixed up. Intervals are wide.
Results data: FIDE Grand Swiss 2025 (Open) game files; V Open Chess Menorca 2026 (info64.org); Aeroflot Open 2026 (chess-results.com). Player names appear as in the published results.
